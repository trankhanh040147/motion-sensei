# Filming guide (send to the player when data quality is poor)

The analysis uses one 2D camera, so the camera angle decides which measurements are possible.

1. **Side view (best for shooting).** Phone at chest height, 4–6 m away, perpendicular to the shooting line, shooting arm facing the camera. Measures knees, elbow angles, timing, ball arc.
2. **Front view (for elbow alignment and drift).** Phone behind the rim or straight in front of the player, about 5 m away. Filming from behind the player also works for shots, but the body hides the ball while dribbling and the shooting hand at release, so dribble analysis and release metrics become approximate.
3. **Player big in the frame**: they should fill at least a third of the frame height. Far-away players make the ball only a few pixels wide and it gets missed.
4. **Whole body in frame for the entire shot**, including the jump and feet on landing, plus some space above the head for the release.
5. **Fixed camera.** Use a tripod or lean the phone against something. Do not follow the player.
6. **60 fps or higher** (iPhone: Settings → Camera → Record Video → 1080p at 60 fps; slow-motion 120/240 fps works too). 30 fps blurs the release.
7. **Light**: good, even light; avoid strong backlight from windows.
8. **Alone in frame**: other people walking through can be mistaken for the player.
9. **Ball contrast**: an orange ball against a plain background tracks best.
10. 10–20 shots per video is enough; 30 s to 3 min.
